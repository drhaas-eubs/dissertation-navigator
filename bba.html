Structure

The six-chapter structure

The BBA follows the same six-chapter structure as the MBA — the word targets are lower. References (7) and appendices (8) complete the document; both are excluded from the word count.

Introduction ~1,400 words · rubric weight 10% (introduction, research question & objectives)

The introduction states the rationale, intent, context and purpose of the research. At the highest band it is compelling and well structured, and it contextualises the research gap so the reader fully understands the purpose and importance of the study. It narrows in a controlled way from context, to problem, to gap, to question.

What this chapter must contain

1.1 Background and context
Establish the real-world business situation that prompted the study — naming the industry, market, organisation or phenomenon. Provide background data (figures, trends, regulatory or competitive developments), each carrying an in-text Harvard citation. Narrow from the general context to the specific problem.
1.2 Problem statement and research gap
State the specific problem or tension in one or two sentences — what is lost, risked or misunderstood if it remains unexamined, and for whom. State what the literature has established and what it has not, naming the gap explicitly. Explain why the gap needs filling.
1.3 Research aim, research question and objectives
State the aim, principal question, subsidiary questions or hypotheses, and objectives — exactly as they will appear in the defence slides and abstract. Objectives are executable actions, not a restatement of the question.
1.4 Scope, delimitations and significance
State the boundaries you deliberately set. Distinguish delimitations (your choices) from limitations (constraints on you, which belong in the final chapter). State the contribution to practice and to the literature.
1.5 Structure of the dissertation
A short passage stating what each chapter does. Composed last, once the chapters exist in final form.

AMBER · CHAPTER 1 SELF-CHECK
  • The introduction narrows logically from context to problem to gap to question.
  • Every factual claim about the industry or market is supported by an in-text citation.
  • The objectives are executable actions, not a restatement of the research question.
  • The scope names an industry, a geography, a population and a time frame.
  • The chapter is written in continuous prose, without bulleted or numbered lists.
Background & Literature Review ~4,200 words · rubric weight 20% (the heaviest criterion)

A critical, structured synthesis of the existing scholarship. Organised by theme and debate, not author by author, it builds toward the research gap your study fills. The heaviest single criterion in the rubric — synthesis rather than catalogue is what earns the marks.

What this chapter must contain

Organise by theme, not author
Cluster sources by the ideas they address, then compare within each cluster. Use theme headings, not author names, as your structure.
Be critical, not descriptive
Evaluate the quality, method and limits of what you cite. Do not report every claim as equally valid; weigh the evidence.
Cite the seminal originators
Cite Ajzen (1991) for TPB, Teece et al. (1997) for dynamic capabilities, and so on — not only recent secondary summaries. Recent sources support; the originator must be present.
Converge on the gap
Every strand converges on the gap your study fills, giving your research questions their justification. If the reader cannot state your gap after reading, it has not done its job.

Methodology ~2,400 words · replicable: what, how and why

The methodology sets out what was done, how, and why, in enough detail to be replicable. Examiners look for coherence down the paradigm chain: a stance and a design that contradict each other is the commonest flag. Every choice is justified against the research question.

What this chapter must contain

Philosophy and approach
Name your research philosophy and reasoning, and justify why the question demands them. Interpretivism with a large anonymous survey is a mismatch examiners spot at once.
Design and methods
State the overall design (case study, survey, mixed) and the specific instruments. Describe administration precisely: how many, how long, recorded how, piloted how.
Sampling
State the target population, technique, achieved size and its logic (power for quant; saturation for qual). Match the generalisation claim to the sample.
Quality and ethics
Address validity and reliability (or trustworthiness) explicitly, with the steps taken. State the signed Ethics Agreement was in place before any data collection.

Findings / Results ~3,500 words · organised by research question

The findings report what the data shows — organised, evidenced and structured by the research questions — but not yet interpreted against the literature. That interpretation is the job of the Discussion. Keep the raw evidence on file.

What this chapter must contain

Structure by research question
Organise the chapter around the research questions, so the reader can see each one answered by the data in turn.
Present, don't interpret
Report the evidence with tables and figures. Save the comparison with prior literature for Chapter 5; here you establish what was found.
Use tables and figures
Tabulation presents structured data efficiently and is separately graded. Lists in running prose are not permitted; tables are expected.
Keep the raw evidence
Transcripts, datasets, coded workbooks and signed consent forms stay on file. 'Findings with no raw data' is an authenticity flag.

Discussion ~1,400 words · standalone · where the analytical marks are won

A standalone chapter, never merged into Findings — that separation is the recurring examiner condition. Here you interpret the findings against the literature and your research questions: what they mean, how they compare with prior work, and what follows.

What this chapter must contain

Interpret against the literature
Bring each finding into conversation with the sources from Chapter 2. Where does it confirm, extend or contradict prior work?
Answer the research questions
Show how the findings address each research question. This is where the analytical marks concentrate.
State the implications
What do the results mean for practice and for theory? Draw out the 'so what' explicitly.
Acknowledge limitations
State the constraints that bound your conclusions honestly — these are limitations (imposed on you), distinct from the delimitations you chose.

Conclusion ~900 words · answer, recommend, limit, extend

The conclusion answers the research questions directly and states the contribution. It introduces no new data. It closes the loop opened in Chapter 1, gives practical recommendations, and points to future research.

What this chapter must contain

Answer the questions directly
Restate and answer each research question, closing the loop with the introduction. No drift from the question posed in Chapter 1.
State the contribution
What is now known that was not known before — to practice, and to the literature? Make the two-audience contribution explicit.
Give recommendations
Practical, evidence-based recommendations that follow from the findings, not generic advice.
Limitations and future research
Acknowledge limitations and suggest specific, well-motivated directions for future work.

Appendix Supporting materials · excluded from the word count

The appendices hold everything that supports the dissertation but sits outside the main word count — the lists generated from your headings, and the raw materials that evidence your research.

8.1 List of Figures
A generated list of every figure in the dissertation, with its number, caption and page. Built from the figure labels — update it before submission so numbers and pages are correct.
8.2 List of Tables
A generated list of every table, with its number, caption and page. Tables are expected and separately graded under organisation; the list is built from the table labels.
8.3 Other materials
The raw evidence and instruments: the questionnaire or interview schedule, participant information and signed consent forms, transcripts, coded workbooks, datasets, and the AI Annex (PowerPoint with one screenshot per source, on a working OneDrive link). This is the audit trail that authenticates your findings.
AMBER · APPENDIX SELF-CHECK
  • List of Figures and List of Tables are generated from labels and updated.
  • The questionnaire or interview schedule is included in full.
  • Signed consent forms and raw transcripts/datasets are retained.
  • The AI Annex link is present, tested and set to "anyone with the link".
  • Appendices are excluded from the introduction-to-conclusion word count.
