Part I · Registration & Proposal

The Dissertation Proposal

Before the dissertation comes the proposal. This is the EUBS Dissertation Proposal / Registration Form, section by section, written so you can draft directly against it. Get this right and the dissertation has a spine; rush it and every later chapter inherits the weakness.

The proposal is submitted first and signed off before you begin. The bibliography here is capped at 20 sources — the 80–100 (MBA) / 50–80 (BBA) range applies to the full dissertation.

Section B

The proposal — seven parts

Proposed Dissertation Title tentative, but precise

The title must be precise enough for the reader to grasp the subject matter from it alone.

What this section must contain

Name the phenomenon, context and, ideally, the relationship studied
Tentative is fine; vague is not
It should foreshadow the research question
Keep it concise and specific

Research Question or Hypothesis ~100 words

State the research interest clearly, defining the question or hypothesis and the main objectives.

What this section must contain

One principal question, clearly answerable
Subsidiary questions or hypotheses if needed
Objectives are executable actions, not restatements
This anchors everything downstream

Review of Literature / Background ~1,000 words

Synthesise the contributions of accredited scholars and show how your research extends them.

What this section must contain

Synthesise, don't summarise source by source
Show what is known and what is missing
State the contribution you intend to make
Cite in full Harvard style

The proposal is graded on coherence, not length. A precise question, a literature background that lands on a genuine gap, and a methodology that visibly fits that question will always beat a long, unfocused draft. Read down one column of the paradigm chain and keep every choice consistent.
