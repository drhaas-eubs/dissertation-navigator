Part III · The Jury Defence

The Jury Defence

A 15–20 minute oral defence. The jury has already read and assessed your dissertation — this is not a presentation of your topic. Use the time to underscore the process, choices and experiences of doing the research, structured as a thumbnail storyboard with a clear message strategy.

Use the official EUBS PowerPoint template. Slides support the defence; they are not its epicentre.
Mindset

It's a defence, not a presentation

The jury members are familiar with the substance of your work. Don't re-present the topic or re-argue the whole thesis. Underscore what you did, why you chose it, and what you learned doing it — and be ready to defend every choice.

What the jury is really testing: ownership. The oral defence rubric rewards a candidate who expands on answers, knows the study's strengths and weaknesses, and shows independent, self-directed thinking. Convincing ownership of the study is the single strongest signal you can give.

Message strategy · Kurt Bostelaar

One message per slide, and know your throughline

A clear message strategy means every slide answers "what is the one thing they should take from this?" — and the slides together form a single throughline a listener can restate afterwards.

Do

  • Lead each slide with its message, not its data.
  • Speak around the slide — don't read it.
  • Match the amount of content to the 15–20 minutes.
  • Keep visuals clean, readable and professional.
  • Rehearse the likely defence questions.

Avoid

  • Superfluous graphics that don't support the point.
  • Reading slides verbatim with no eye contact.
  • Re-presenting the whole dissertation.
  • Dense, over-adorned slides.
  • Vague answers that dodge your own choices.
House note. The "message strategy" here is a synthesis of documented communication principles, presented as house guidance. If you have a specific Bostelaar handout, use it in place of this framing.
